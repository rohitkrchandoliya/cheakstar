What Is Bug Bounty? A Beginner's Honest Starting Guide

30 Oct 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

What bug bounty actually is

Bug bounty is a simple arrangement. A company publishes a programme that says: "These are our systems. If you find a security flaw in them, report it to us responsibly, and we will thank you - sometimes with money, always within the rules written on the programme page."

That is the whole idea. Everything else - the platforms, the leaderboards, the tools - is decoration around this one agreement: permission in exchange for honest, well-reported findings.

What bug bounty is not

It is not a licence to test anything you like. A programme covers only the assets listed in its scope. Testing a system outside that scope, or a company with no programme at all, is unauthorised access in most legal systems - the opposite of what a security career is built on.

It is also not a promised income, and nobody honest will promise you one. Most beginners find nothing reportable for weeks. The people who last in this field treat the early months as paid-in-knowledge practice: every rejected report and every empty day teaches something, if it is written down and studied.

And it is not magic. There is no secret tool that finds bugs while you sleep. There is a method: understand how the application works, look where trust is assumed, test carefully, and prove what you found.

The honest order of learning

Step 1 - Foundations. Learn how the web works: HTTP requests and responses, cookies and sessions, how browsers and servers talk. Without this, tools are just buttons.

Step 2 - One vulnerability class at a time. Start with a single class - for example, access control flaws - and understand it deeply: what it is, why it happens, how to prove it, how to fix it.

Step 3 - Legal practice. Use the free labs and training ranges the community provides. Practise where mistakes are welcome.

Step 4 - Read real reports. Publicly disclosed reports show what a strong submission looks like: clear steps, honest impact, repeatable evidence.

Step 5 - Join a programme and start small. Pick one programme, read its scope and rules twice, and test only what it lists.

The report is half the skill

New hunters obsess over finding. Experienced hunters obsess over reporting. A report that a tired engineer can reproduce in five minutes - exact steps, exact evidence, exact impact - is worth more than a vague claim about a severe-sounding bug. Write like a professional from your very first report.

A final word of encouragement

Everyone you see succeeding in bug bounty began exactly where you are: reading an article like this one and wondering if it is really for them. The difference is not talent. It is a process, followed patiently.
