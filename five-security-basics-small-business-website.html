Five Security Basics Every Small Business Website Needs

10 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

Your website is a shopfront that never closes

A small business website works nights and weekends, takes enquiries while you sleep, and represents you to strangers deciding whether to trust you. It is also, quietly, a target: automated scanners sweep the internet continuously, testing every site they find for known weaknesses. They do not care how small the business is. They care how open the door is.

The encouraging truth: most of what protects a small site is basic, cheap in effort, and done once. These are the five checks a professional runs first.

1. Updates, treated as routine

Most small websites run on a content platform with add-ons - themes, plugins, extensions. Nearly every serious compromise of such sites walks through an outdated component with a publicly known flaw. Switch on automatic updates where the platform offers them, remove add-ons you no longer use (unused is not harmless; it is unmaintained), and glance at the update screen monthly. Our article on software updates explains why the timing matters so much.

2. Logins that resist guessing

The admin login is the master key, and it gets tested constantly. Give every person their own account - never one shared admin login - with a unique password stored in a password manager, and two-factor authentication on top. Remove accounts of people who have left. Rename nothing and hide nothing; simply make the door strong and the keys counted.

3. Backups you have actually tested

A backup that has never been restored is a hope, not a backup. Keep automatic backups that live somewhere separate from the website itself, and once - just once - practise restoring one, so the first attempt does not happen during a crisis. If the worst day comes, a tested backup turns a disaster into an afternoon of inconvenience.

4. The padlock, and forms that behave

Your site should load over HTTPS - the padlock in the browser - so information between visitors and your site is encrypted. Most hosts now include the certificate free; if yours does not, that is a question worth asking them. While you are there, look at your contact and enquiry forms: they should ask only for what you genuinely need. Every extra field you collect is data you are responsible for protecting.

5. Someone whose job it is to look

Security fails quietly when it is nobody's task. It does not need to be a department: one named person who checks updates monthly, reviews user accounts quarterly, and knows who to call when something looks wrong. For many small businesses, that review is also where an authorised, professional security check fits - an outside pair of eyes, with written permission and a clear report, catching what routine misses. It is the service we describe on our own site, and the standard any provider should meet.

None of these five require expertise to start - only the decision that the shopfront deserves the same care as the shop.
