Your Home Router: The Security Device You Forgot You Owned

21 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

The most important computer you never think about

Your home has one device that every other device trusts completely: the router. Phones, laptops, televisions and visitors all reach the internet through it. It decides who joins your network and it stands between your home and the rest of the world. In every practical sense, it is a security device.

Yet in most homes it still runs exactly as it left the factory - set up in five hurried minutes on the day the connection arrived, and untouched since. Factory settings are chosen for easy installation, not for years of guarding a house.

Job one: change the admin password

A router has two passwords, and people regularly confuse them. The Wi-Fi password lets devices join the network. The admin password controls the router itself - and it often ships as a default printed in a manual that anyone can download. Whoever holds the admin password can rearrange the whole network: where traffic goes, who is allowed in, everything.

Log in to the router's settings once - its address and the route in are printed on a sticker on the device - and change the admin password to a unique one stored in your password manager. Ten minutes, once, removes the most embarrassing open door in the house.

Job two: keep its software updated

Routers run software, and that software is repaired over time just like a phone's. Some routers update themselves if you switch the option on; others need you to press a button in their official app or settings page every few months. Either way, make it a small habit, a few times a year. An updated router has had its known weaknesses closed; an untouched one keeps every weakness it shipped with, publicly documented.

Job three: a proper Wi-Fi password and a guest door

Your Wi-Fi password should be long, unique, and not the one the installer suggested or the provider printed, if you have the option to change it. Then, if your router offers a guest network, switch it on for visitors. Guests get internet; your own devices - especially the computer holding your files - stay on the private side of the wall. The same guest network is a sensible home for smart bulbs, cameras and other gadgets, keeping them apart from the devices that hold your real work.

Job four: turn off what you do not use

Routers offer convenience features that also widen the door: remote management from the internet, and automatic connection features designed to make pairing effortless. If you do not use them, switch them off in settings. Every feature you close is one less way in, and you will never miss the ones you never used.

A fifteen-minute annual ritual

None of this is a weekly chore. Change the admin password once. Switch on updates once. Glance at the settings a few times a year, the way you would check the locks on a house you have lived in a while. The router asks for almost nothing - but everything in your home stands behind it, so the little it asks is worth giving.
