Fake Login Pages: Telling the Real Door From a Painted One

20 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

A painted door can look perfect

Imagine a door painted on a wall, handle and all. From a distance it is convincing. It only fails when you look closely - or try the handle. Fake login pages work the same way. The logo is right, the colours are right, the wording is copied word for word. Everything is genuine except the one thing that matters: where the page actually lives.

Understanding that single fact turns a frightening attack into a manageable one.

Why the copy is so good

Attackers do not design these pages; they clone them. The real page's code and images can be copied wholesale, so judging by appearance is hopeless by design. The page then sits on an address the attacker controls, receives whatever you type, and often forwards you to the real site afterwards so nothing seems wrong. Your password was harvested in the middle of a completely normal-looking experience.

The check that still works: the address

A domain name is the one part the attacker cannot copy. Your bank's real address is fixed. The fake must differ - by a swapped or doubled letter, an extra word joined with a dash, a familiar name attached to an unfamiliar ending, or the real name appearing as a subdomain of something else entirely.

Read the domain slowly, from the right side of the address. The true owner of a site is named just before the ending - the part after the last dot before any slashes. Everything before that can be decoration the attacker added to reassure you. This takes five seconds and a little practice, and it defeats copies of any quality.

The warning signs around the page

Even a perfect page arrives inside a story, and the story usually leaks. The link came in a message pushing urgency. The address was hidden behind a shortener or a button. Your browser, arriving at a site you use daily, does not offer your saved login the way it normally does. Any one of these is a reason to stop, close the tab, and start again by your own route.

The habit that defeats every copy

Here is the deeper defence, and it does not depend on your eyesight at all: never arrive at an important login through a link someone sent you. Type the address yourself, use your own bookmark, or open the official app. When you always enter by a route you chose, a painted door can be built anywhere in the world and you will simply never walk up to it.

A password manager quietly helps here too: it fills your password only on the real domain it saved it from. If your manager refuses to offer the login it usually provides, treat that silence as a warning worth listening to.

Teach it to one more person

Attackers aim these pages at whoever is busiest, newest or least technical - often not you, but someone in your family. Show them the painted-door idea and the own-route habit once, in calm conditions. It is a five-minute conversation that can quietly protect every account they own.
