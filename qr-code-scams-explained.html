QR Code Scams: The Sticker Is the Attack

23 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

A link you cannot read

Every other link on the internet shows its destination. You can read an address, hover over it, judge it. A QR code shows you a pattern of squares. The destination is real, but it stays hidden until your phone decodes it - and by design, phones decode eagerly and ask few questions.

Scammers did not miss this. The QR code has quietly become one of the simplest delivery mechanisms for phishing and payment fraud, precisely because it skips the moment where a careful person would normally pause.

How the common scams work

The sticker swap. A genuine code is printed somewhere public - a parking payment sign, a restaurant table card, a charging point, a poster. The scammer prints their own code on a sticker and pastes it over the original. Everyone who scans pays the scammer, or lands on a lookalike page that collects card details. The swap takes seconds and can survive for weeks.

The code in the message. An email or message arrives with a code to scan "to verify your account" or "to receive a refund". Scanning opens a convincing fake login page. The trick adds a step that feels technical and official - and moves you from the device where you might have checked the sender to one where you did not.

The too-good offer. Posters and social posts advertising prizes, jobs or deals through a code, leading to pages that ask for personal details or small "processing" payments. The code is not the scam; it is the curtain in front of it.

The three checks that defeat most of it

Check the physical code. On signs and tables, run a finger over the code. A sticker sitting on top of another sticker, or a code that looks newer and glossier than everything around it, deserves suspicion. Ask staff - businesses are usually grateful to be told.

Read the address after scanning. Before the page fully loads or any payment begins, your phone shows the address the code contained. Read it the way you would read any link: is this the real domain of the organisation, letter for letter? If the code on a parking sign opens an address that is plainly not the parking company's, stop.

Keep your own route for payments and logins. A code can inform you; it should rarely be the doorway. For anything involving money or accounts, open the official app or type the official address yourself. If the offer is real, it will exist there too.

A note on balance

Most QR codes are exactly what they claim to be - menus, tickets, genuine payment points. The goal of this article is not fear of small black squares. It is restoring the pause that the format removed. A link you cannot read deserves one extra second of reading once your phone reveals it. That second is the whole defence.
