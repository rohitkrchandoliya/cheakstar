How to Spot a Phishing Message in Under a Minute

31 Oct 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

Why phishing still works

Phishing survives because it does not attack computers first. It attacks attention. A message arrives dressed as your bank, your courier or your employer, carrying a feeling of emergency: act now, or lose something. Under that pressure, careful people click careless links.

The defence is not suspicion of everything. It is a short, calm routine - five checks that take under a minute.

Check 1: The sender address, letter by letter

Attackers register addresses that look right at a glance: a swapped letter, an extra word, a familiar name on an unfamiliar domain. Read the full address slowly. The display name can say anything; the address cannot hide as easily.

Check 2: Where the link really goes

On a computer, hover before you click. On a phone, press and hold to preview the destination. If the visible text says one site and the address leads somewhere else, the message has failed the test. Legitimate organisations do not send you to lookalike domains.

Check 3: The urgency itself

"Your account will be closed today." "A parcel fee is pending, pay in one hour." Real institutions rarely demand instant action through a message link. Treat urgency as evidence - the more a message rushes you, the slower you should go.

Check 4: What it asks for

Passwords, card numbers, one-time codes: no genuine bank, platform or employer asks for these by message or email. A request for a secret is itself the red flag, whatever the story around it is.

Check 5: The independent route

If a message claims something important, verify it without touching the message at all. Open the official app or type the official address yourself. If the claim is real, it will be waiting for you there. If it exists only inside that one message, it was never real.

The habit that defeats the rest

Some phishing is excellent - correct spelling, perfect branding, a believable story. Against that, individual checks can fail. The habit that still protects you: never complete a sensitive action from inside a message. Always arrive at the service by your own route. A message can inform you; it should never be the doorway.

Share these checks with the least technical person in your family. They are the ones the attackers are really aiming at.
