Selling Your Old Phone, Safely: An Erase-It-Properly Guide

24 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

The phone you are selling is an archive

Think about what a used phone actually contains. Years of photos and chats. Email signed in. Banking and payment apps, logged in or one reset away from it. Saved passwords in the browser. Notes, documents, location history, health records. A modern phone is not a device with some data on it - it is one of the most complete records of a life that has ever existed.

Selling it, gifting it or trading it in is perfectly sensible. Doing so without erasing it properly is how private lives quietly change hands. This guide is the careful half-hour that prevents that.

Step 1: Back up what you still need

Before anything is erased, move what matters: photos, contacts, documents, chat backups, authenticator apps. Check the backup completed - open it and look. Erasing first and discovering an incomplete backup afterwards is a loss no buyer can refund you. Take your time on this step; every later step is irreversible on purpose.

Step 2: Sign out of the accounts that own the phone

Phones are tied to a main account - the account that runs the app store, the cloud backup and the find-my-device service. Sign out of it on the device itself. This does two jobs: it removes the link between the phone and your account, and it releases the device so the next owner can set it up as theirs. Also sign out of email, banking and social apps individually where the option exists - belt and braces cost minutes.

While you are here, remove the phone from your list of trusted devices on your important accounts, so it can no longer receive sign-in approvals for you.

Step 3: Remove the physical pieces

Take out the SIM card - it carries your number and sometimes stored contacts. Remove any memory card; it does not get erased by the phone's reset and often holds photos and downloads. These travel with you, not with the phone.

Step 4: Run the factory reset

In the settings, find the reset option and choose the full factory reset - the one described as erasing all data. Modern phones encrypt their storage by default; the reset destroys the encryption keys, which makes the data on the device unreadable rather than merely hidden. This is why the built-in reset, on a modern phone, is the correct tool - no special software is needed.

When the phone restarts to its welcome screen, as if new, the job is done. Set nothing up on it. That untouched welcome screen is also the most reassuring sight for a buyer.

Step 5: The final sweep

Check the places outside the phone: your cloud account should no longer list the device, your accounts' device lists should not show it, and any paired gadgets - watches, earbuds - should be unpaired. Then the phone can go, holding nothing of yours but its scratches.

The principle to keep

Deleting files is tidying; resetting is erasing. A deleted photo often still exists in storage until overwritten, which is why this guide sends you to the reset, not the delete key. Half an hour, once per phone, and the archive closes. Your next phone starts your story fresh - make sure the old one stops telling it.
