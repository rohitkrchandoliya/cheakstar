Security Questions Are Just Extra Passwords

25 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

The forgotten second password

Set a strong password, switch on two-factor authentication, and your account feels finished. But many services keep a quieter door: the security question. Forget your password, answer "What was the name of your first school?" correctly, and the account opens.

Treat that answer with clear eyes and it is obvious what it is: a second password. An unusually weak one - short, guessable, and chosen from facts rather than invented secrets.

Why the answers are weak

They are facts, and facts get published. First schools appear in social profiles and alumni posts. Maiden names appear in family trees and wedding announcements. Favourite foods, pets and childhood streets appear in the playful quizzes people answer publicly without a second thought. An attacker does not need to guess your answers; they need to research them.

They cannot be changed easily. A leaked password can be replaced in a minute. A leaked first-school answer is true forever, and it may guard several accounts at once if you answered honestly everywhere.

They are asked at the worst moment. Security questions guard the recovery path - the exact path an attacker takes when the password has stopped them. A strong front door with a researched-answer back door is, in practice, a house with a weak back door.

The fix: answers that are not answers

Nothing in the design says your answer must be true. The service stores a string and later checks that you type the same string. So give it a string that works like a password.

When a service asks for your first school, answer with an invented one - or better, with a random phrase. "Correct" stops meaning factually right; it means matching what you set. A researcher hunting your real first school will find it, type it, and fail.

Store these answers in your password manager, in the notes of the matching account entry, exactly as you store the password itself. That pairing matters: a recovery answer you cannot find when you genuinely need it is its own kind of lockout, so keep the manager entry complete and backed up.

Where you will still meet honest questions

Some services choose the questions for you and offer no custom option. There, pick the question whose answer is least documented in your life - avoid anything a public profile, a family post or a form you once filled could reveal. And if the same service offers two-factor authentication or a recovery email you control strongly instead, prefer those paths and treat the question as the last resort it should be.

The habit in one line

A password is a secret you invent; a security-question answer should be one too. Move yours from the first category to the second, store them properly, and the quiet back door gets the same strong lock as the front.
