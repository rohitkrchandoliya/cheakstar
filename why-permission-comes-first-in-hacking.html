Why Permission Comes First in Hacking - Always

2 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

One line separates research from crime

The techniques overlap. The tools overlap. Sometimes even the curiosity overlaps. What separates a security researcher from a criminal is a single thing: authorisation. Testing a system you own, or one whose owner has given you written permission and a defined scope, is research. The identical test without that permission is an offence in most countries - including under India's Information Technology Act.

This is not fine print. It is the foundation the entire profession stands on.

What real permission looks like

Permission is specific, not vibes. A bug bounty programme's published scope is permission - for the assets listed, by the methods allowed, under the rules written there. A signed engagement letter for a security assessment is permission - for the client, the systems and the dates it names. A lab environment built for practice is permission - inside the lab.

A friend saying "sure, try my website" in a chat message is a grey area; professionals get it in writing with a scope. A company's public website being reachable is not permission. Finding a flaw accidentally is not permission to explore it further - the professional move is to stop, document the minimum needed, and report it responsibly.

Why professionals guard this line

Three reasons. First, the law: unauthorised access can end a career before it begins, and "I was only learning" is not a defence. Second, trust: this industry runs on it; a researcher known for respecting scope gets more invitations, more programmes and more work. Third, the work itself is better inside the line - inside authorised programmes you can test thoroughly, ask questions, and get rewarded, instead of looking over your shoulder.

The beginner's safe playgrounds

You never need to break this rule to learn. Deliberately vulnerable practice applications, community training ranges, capture-the-flag events and bug bounty programmes all exist precisely so beginners can attack real-looking systems legally. Everything you need for your first year of learning is available inside the line.

Our house rule

At Cheakstar, permission is not a step in the process. It is step zero - before tools, before recon, before anything. Learn the skill. Respect the boundary. That order never changes.
