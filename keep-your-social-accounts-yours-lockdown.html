Keep Your Social Accounts Yours: A Practical Lock-Down

12 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

Why ordinary accounts get stolen

People assume attackers want celebrities. In practice, stolen ordinary accounts are a business: a hijacked profile with real friends and a real history can spread scams that strangers trust, message contacts asking for money, or be sold as a ready-made audience. Small accounts are often easier targets precisely because their owners never expected to be one.

The lock-down below takes about twenty minutes per account, once. It is the same routine whether the account has thirty followers or thirty thousand.

Start with the password, and make it lonely

Change the account's password to a unique one, generated and remembered by a password manager. Social passwords are among the most reused on the internet, which means among the most leaked. If yours appears on any other service, assume it is known. While you are there, check the email address attached to the account - recovery flows go to it, so it must be an inbox you control and protect just as carefully.

Turn on two-factor, the app way

Every major platform offers two-factor authentication. Use an authenticator app rather than text messages where you can choose: codes by message depend on your mobile number staying yours, and numbers get hijacked in ways an app on your device does not. Save the backup codes the platform offers, in your password manager, the day you switch it on - not the day you are locked out wishing you had.

Review who else is inside

Accounts accumulate doors. In the security settings, look at three lists: active sessions and logged-in devices (remove anything you do not recognise), connected third-party apps (revoke the quiz tools and forgotten games from years ago), and - for pages and business accounts - the list of people with admin or editor roles. Every entry on those lists is a way in that bypasses your password entirely, which is why they matter as much as the password itself.

Decide what strangers can see

Account theft is only half the subject; the other half is what a profile quietly publishes. Birth dates, hometown, workplace, family names, the school in the photo background - these are the raw materials of impersonation and of guessing security answers. You do not have to hide your life; simply check the audience on personal details and old posts, and set a deliberate default instead of an inherited one. Our digital footprint guide shows what a stranger can assemble in ten minutes, and it is a bracing read.

Agree on the family signal

If your account ever does get misused, the damage runs through your contacts: "I am in trouble, send money, do not call." A standing arrangement defeats it - in this family, urgent money requests are always verified by a voice call to a known number, whatever the message claims. Our family safety guide describes setting exactly this kind of agreement up kindly and well.

Twenty minutes, honestly done, moves your accounts into the category attackers skip: the ones where the easy doors are all, visibly, shut.
