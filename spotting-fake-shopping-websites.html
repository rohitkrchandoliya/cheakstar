That Store Looks Real. Is It? Spotting Fake Shopping Websites

9 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

Built in a day, designed to be believed

A fake shopping website is not a sloppy page with spelling mistakes. Modern ones are polished: real product photographs borrowed from genuine stores, familiar logos, working search boxes, even fake reviews with names and stars. What is missing is everything behind the shop window - a real business, real stock, and any intention of sending you anything.

The business model is simple. Advertise heavily on social media, harvest payments and card details for a few days, vanish, and reopen under a new name. Your defence is a short inspection routine that takes two minutes.

Check 1: The address, read properly

The store's name in its logo is decoration; the web address is identity. Fake stores hide behind addresses that nearly match a famous brand - extra words, swapped letters, unusual endings. Read the address the way our guide to reading a link teaches: the real name is the part just before the ending, and everything before that can be costume. If the address is a jumble of words and hyphens trading on a brand, close the tab.

Check 2: Prices that end the conversation too quickly

Every market has a floor. A current phone at one-tenth of its price everywhere else is not a discovery; it is the bait itself. Scammers price at the exact point where excitement beats caution - unbelievable, but just believable enough to hurry you. When a price looks like a mistake, assume it is one - theirs, on purpose.

Check 3: The business behind the glass

Genuine stores answer basic questions somewhere on the site: a physical address, a working phone number, a returns policy that names a real process. Fake stores offer a contact form and silence. Search the store's name together with the word "reviews" or "scam" in a separate tab - a site running this week on borrowed trust usually has a trail of complaints already, and a site with no history at all deserves the same caution.

Check 4: How it wants to be paid

This is the loudest signal. Fake stores push payment methods with no way back: direct transfer, payment to a personal account, or cash-on-delivery that quietly converts to prepaid. They avoid cards and established payment platforms because those carry dispute processes. Treat any store that steers you away from protected payment as a store that has told you its plan.

If you already paid

Act while the trail is warm. If you paid by card or through a payment platform, contact the provider immediately and dispute the transaction - speed genuinely matters here. If card details were entered on the site itself, ask your bank about replacing the card, and watch the account closely for small test charges. Report the site on the national cybercrime portal; these storefronts fall faster when reports arrive in clusters.

Real bargains exist. They live on real stores, at believable discounts, behind addresses that check out. Two minutes of inspection is all that separates shopping from donating.
