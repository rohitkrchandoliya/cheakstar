Password Habits That Actually Work (and Take Ten Minutes)

29 Oct 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

The real problem is reuse

Ask how accounts get taken over and the honest answer is boring: a password from one leaked site gets tried everywhere else. The victim used the same password for a shopping site and their email — the shopping site leaked, and the email fell with it. No genius hacking required.

This is why the strongest password advice is not about clever characters. It is about habits. Three of them do most of the work.

Habit 1: One password per important account

Your email account is the master key: password resets for nearly everything flow through it. Give it a unique password used nowhere else. Then do the same for banking and your main social accounts. A password manager makes this effortless — it remembers so you do not have to, and it generates long random passwords you could never memorise anyway.

Habit 2: Two-factor authentication, everywhere it exists

Two-factor authentication means a thief needs your password and your phone or authenticator app. Turn it on for email first, then banking, then social media. Prefer an authenticator app over SMS when you have the choice; keep your backup codes somewhere safe and offline.

Habit 3: Passphrases for the few passwords you type

For the rare passwords you must remember and type — your device login, your password manager itself — use a passphrase: four or five unrelated words joined together. Length beats complexity. A long phrase you remember is stronger in practice than a short symbol puzzle you end up writing on a sticky note.

What to stop doing today

Stop reusing one favourite password with small variations. Stop saving passwords in plain phone notes. Stop sharing passwords over chat “just for a minute”. And stop believing a password alone still protects anything important in 2026 — it does not; the second factor is what completes it.

Ten minutes, real protection

Here is the whole assignment: install a password manager, change your email password to a unique generated one, and switch on two-factor authentication for email and banking. Ten minutes, once. Attackers count on you postponing it. Be the account that is not worth their time.
