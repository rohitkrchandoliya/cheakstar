Software Updates: The Security Habit Hiding in Plain Sight

30 Oct 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

The flaw was already fixed

Read the reports after almost any large attack and a pattern appears. The attackers did not discover a brand-new weakness. They used a known flaw - one the software maker had already found, already fixed, and already released a repair for. The victims had simply not installed it yet.

This is why experienced security people rank the humble software update among the most powerful defences an ordinary person has. It is not exciting. It just quietly closes doors that attackers are actively walking through.

What an update actually does

Software is written by humans, and humans miss things. An update is the maker's correction round: it repairs flaws, including security flaws that would let someone read your data, install unwanted software, or take control of the device. When an update is labelled a security update, it means at least one of those flaws was serious enough to name publicly.

Delaying an update does not keep your device as it is. It keeps the flaw as it is - while the details of that flaw become public knowledge, studied by exactly the people you least want studying them.

Switch on automatic updates, everywhere

The reliable fix is to stop relying on memory. Turn on automatic updates in four places:

Your phone. In the system settings, allow system updates and app updates to install automatically. This covers the operating system and everything from the app store.

Your computer. Windows, macOS and Linux all offer automatic updates. Enable them, including the option to update other installed software where it exists.

Your browser. Browsers update themselves by default - leave that setting alone, and restart the browser when it asks, since many updates only finish applying after a restart.

Your other devices. Routers, smart TVs and similar gadgets also receive updates, often hidden in their settings menus. Check them a few times a year; the router matters most, because it guards the door to everything else in the house.

Real updates never arrive by pop-up

One warning matters here. Attackers fake update notices - a web page suddenly claims your player, browser or system is out of date and offers a download. That download is the attack. Genuine updates come only through the device's own settings or the official app store, never from a banner on a website. When in doubt, close the page and open your settings yourself. If an update is real, it will be waiting there.

A rhythm a beginner can keep

You do not need a schedule worthy of an IT department. Connect your phone to its charger and Wi-Fi at night and let it handle the rest. Restart your computer once a week so pending updates finish. Glance at the router and other devices every few months. That is the entire system.

Beginners often ask which advanced tool to learn first. Here is a less glamorous answer: the person whose devices are simply up to date is already harder to attack than most of the internet. Start there, and build the rest on top.
