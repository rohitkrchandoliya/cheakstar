Using AI Chatbots Safely: What Never to Paste

8 Nov 2026 · Cheakstar Blog

The chat box feels private. Treat it like a public desk.

AI chatbots answer in a friendly, one-to-one tone, and that tone invites honesty. People paste contracts, medical reports, office documents and personal messages into them the way they would confide in a diary. It is worth pausing on what actually happens: your text is sent to a company's servers, processed there, and - depending on the service and its settings - may be stored, reviewed for quality, or used to improve future models.

None of that makes chatbots bad tools. They are excellent ones. It simply means they should be used the way you would use a helpful stranger at a public desk: ask freely, but choose what you show them.

What never to paste

Passwords, codes and keys. No chatbot ever needs your password, a login code, or an API key to help you. If a task seems to require one, that is the signal to stop and do the task another way.

Full financial and identity details. Complete card numbers, bank account numbers with branch codes, and government identity numbers do not belong in a chat. If you need help with a document that contains them, remove or type over those parts first - the advice rarely depends on the real digits.

Other people's private information. A customer's email list, a colleague's personal details, a friend's private messages. If it is not yours to share, a chatbot is still sharing.

Confidential work material. Many employers treat pasted source code, unreleased plans and internal documents as a disclosure, whatever the tool. When in doubt, ask your employer which tools are approved for work content - or keep work off personal accounts entirely.

How to ask safely, and still get the help

Most questions survive anonymising. Replace real names with placeholders - "Customer A", "the supplier". Replace real numbers with similar dummy numbers when the exact figure does not matter. Paste the one paragraph you are stuck on rather than the whole private document. You will find the quality of help barely changes, while your exposure drops to nearly nothing.

Check the settings once

Most major chatbots offer a setting to stop your conversations being used for training, and controls to delete conversation history. Find them, set them the way you prefer, and clear old chats occasionally - especially any where you pasted more than you meant to. Five minutes, once, is a fair price for the habit.

Remember the other direction, too

Safety runs both ways. A chatbot's confident answer is not automatically a correct one: it can invent facts, dates and rules with perfect grammar. For anything that matters - money, health, law, an important message you are about to send - treat the answer as a draft to verify, not a verdict to obey.

Used with those two habits - careful about what goes in, careful about what you trust coming out - AI chatbots are what they should be: powerful, ordinary tools that never learn more about your life than you intended to teach them.
